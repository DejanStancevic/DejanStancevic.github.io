Hi, I’m Dejan.

I’m a PhD researcher at the Donders Centre for Cognition, Radboud University, working with Luca Ambrogioni. I use information theory and statistical physics to understand generative models: how they turn noise into structure, what they learn from data, and where computation matters most.

Research

My research uses information theory to understand generative models. I am particularly interested in these questions:

  • What can information theory tell us about how generative models work?
  • What are the trade-offs between generative frameworks, such as autoregression and diffusion, and what does each make possible?
  • How do the order and amount of computation shape the generative process?

Much of my work so far has studied neural networks from the outside, through their trajectories, samples, and information flow. I am increasingly interested in what their learned weights encode, how they process information internally, and how training data shapes generalization and creativity. Our recent work on model information investigates one part of this picture: how generated samples depend on the training dataset.

Information-Theoretic Scaling Laws for Generative Diffusion Training

Druv Pai*, Dejan Stančević*, Yi Ma, Luca Ambrogioni · arXiv

Can we predict memorization, generalization, and sample quality in diffusion models by measuring how much their generated samples depend on the training dataset? We introduce an information-theoretic perspective and uncover a predictive scaling law.

2026

The Entropic Signature of Class Speciation in Diffusion Models

Florian Handke*, Dejan Stančević*, Felix Koulischer, Thomas Demeester, Luca Ambrogioni · ICML

Peaks in the class-conditional entropy rate help locate speciation times: narrow windows when samples move from semantic ambiguity to class commitment. The same approach tracks decisions at different levels of abstraction.

2026

The Information Dynamics of Generative Diffusion

Dejan Stančević, Luca Ambrogioni · Entropy

We connect conditional entropy with symmetry breaking and finite-time Lyapunov behavior along diffusion trajectories. We show that pathwise entropy tells us about speciation and decision times along generative paths. Together, these results show the importance of conditional entropy for understanding diffusion generation.

2025

Entropic Time Schedulers for Generative Diffusion Models

Dejan Stančević, Florian Handke, Luca Ambrogioni · NeurIPS · Code

We explore a principled way to distribute computation during generation so that each time interval contributes the same amount of information about the final sample.
